What is market structure?

Market structure is a way of describing how price moves between meaningful swing highs and swing lows. Instead of treating every candle as an isolated event, structure asks a larger question: where is price moving, and which important levels is it respecting or breaking?

Bullish structure

A simplified bullish structure is characterized by price forming higher highs and higher lows. A new high demonstrates expansion, while a pullback that remains above the previous important low can preserve the broader structure.

Bearish structure

A simplified bearish structure works in the opposite direction. Price forms lower lows and lower highs, showing that downward expansion continues to control the structure being observed.

MOE AI CONNECTION: Moe AI does not need to predict every candle. The scanner first needs a consistent framework for identifying meaningful structure before evaluating a potential setup.

Why timeframe matters

A chart can appear bullish on one timeframe and bearish on another. That is not necessarily a contradiction. Different timeframes represent different layers of market structure. Moe AI therefore records the timeframe associated with a setup instead of treating all price movement as one structure.

What is a Break of Structure?

A Break of Structure, often shortened to BOS, occurs when price moves through a meaningful structural level defined by the system being used. The important word is meaningful. Not every tiny move above or below a nearby candle should automatically be treated as a significant structural break.

Why systematic definitions matter

Two people can look at the same chart and choose different swing points. A systematic scanner needs explicit rules. Those rules determine which previous structure matters, what qualifies as a break, and whether the resulting move should become a candidate setup.

MOE AI CONNECTION: The Moe AI strategy searches for a meaningful breakout candle relative to previous price structure. The purpose is to turn a visual chart idea into a repeatable rule that software can evaluate.

Break does not mean guaranteed continuation

A structural break does not guarantee that price will continue in the same direction. Price can break, reverse, fail, or create a false breakout. That is why Moe AI uses invalidation levels and records outcomes instead of assuming every detected structure must succeed.

Expansion and retracement

Markets rarely move in one uninterrupted line. After an expansion, price can retrace part of the previous movement. Fibonacci retracement ratios provide one method for measuring the depth of that return.

Ratios used by the Moe AI strategy

The strategy framework uses 0.236, 0.382, 0.500, 0.618 and 0.786 as reference levels inside the measured move. These are not magical prices and they do not predict what the market must do. They provide standardized coordinates that a rules-based system can use consistently.

MOE AI CONNECTION: After a qualifying movement, the scanner can map a predefined retracement area. This gives the system an explicit potential entry zone instead of using an undefined instruction such as “buy the dip.”

Zones instead of certainty

A retracement level should be understood as a measurement, not a guarantee. The market may never reach it, may move directly through it, or may interact with the zone before invalidating the setup.

A setup becomes measurable when its entry, invalidation and target are explicitly defined.

A setup needs boundaries

A market idea without defined boundaries is difficult to evaluate. Moe AI therefore describes a setup using explicit coordinates.

Entry

The entry represents the price or price range where the rules consider the setup actionable or eligible for monitoring.

Invalidation

Invalidation identifies the condition or price that means the original setup logic is no longer valid according to the strategy. This is conceptually important because a systematic model must be able to say when it is wrong.

Target

A target provides a predefined reference for evaluating favorable movement. Targets should be recorded rather than reconstructed later after the outcome is already known.

KEY IDEA: A historical result is meaningful only when the original entry, invalidation and target were defined before the outcome was known.

Every market setup exists under uncertainty. Potential reward does not remove risk.

Signals are not certainty

A scanner can detect a condition that matches its rules. It cannot guarantee what happens next. Market conditions change, liquidity changes, volatility changes, and historical patterns do not force future outcomes.

Risk-reward

Risk-reward compares the distance or amount potentially lost with the distance or amount potentially gained. A favorable ratio does not automatically mean a setup is good. Probability, execution, fees, slippage and changing market conditions also matter.

Leverage

Leverage magnifies exposure. That means it can magnify both gains and losses. High leverage can cause very small price movements to produce disproportionately large changes in account equity.

From idea to engine

The purpose of Moe AI is not simply to display a bullish or bearish label. Its value comes from turning a trading concept into a sequence of explicit rules that software can repeat.

Scan

The engine examines configured markets and timeframes for structures that satisfy the strategy rules.

Filter

Not every movement should become a signal. Filters help reject conditions that do not meet the strategy definition.

Map

When a candidate qualifies, the engine can calculate the relevant entry region, invalidation and targets.

Watch

A setup has a lifecycle. It can remain waiting, become active, reach a target, be invalidated, or expire.

Record

Permanent signal IDs and stored outcomes allow performance to be evaluated later without rewriting history. That distinction is critical for any serious systematic market tool.

THE BIG PICTURE: Moe AI is designed around explainability. The goal is for a user to understand why a setup exists, what would invalidate it, and what eventually happened.
